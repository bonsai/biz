# 連載企画スキル

## Goal
Create a distinctive, researchable episode plan that uses one public figure as a lens for one contemporary question. The figure is not an authority persona and must not be portrayed as having discussed modern AI.

## Workflow
1. Read `00-contents.md`, `AGENTS.md`, and the relevant `agents/<slug>.md` definition.
2. Identify the concrete present-day tension: a dilemma, a failed assumption, an unresolved choice, or a problem people actually experience.
3. Select one figure whose distinctive concept helps reveal that tension. Do not select someone merely because they are famous.
4. Write the central question as one sentence. Make sure it is not just a rephrasing of an existing episode.
5. Define the figure's unique contribution and explicitly state which neighboring agent owns adjacent questions.
6. Separate three layers: historically grounded ideas, contemporary interpretation, and claims that need verification.
7. Choose one recognizable real-world scenario, one counterargument or failure case, and one practical way to test the episode's hypothesis.
8. Build an audio-friendly outline: opening situation, necessary historical idea, contemporary application, human/AI relationship, practical implication, final question.
9. Propose a title that names the tension rather than promising a generic biography.
10. Update the episode table only after the concept is distinct enough to justify a new entry.

## Topic lenses
Choose one main question per episode; do not attempt to answer all of these at once:
- Intelligence: how do producing answers, setting goals, learning from experience, and acting in context differ?
- Work: how do tasks, outcomes, employment, income, contribution, and self-realization differ?
- Complementarity: what new activity becomes possible when people and AI contribute different strengths?
- Delegation: distinguish technical capability from ethical or institutional legitimacy, responsibility, and the value of continued human experience.
- Self-correction: who defines goals, identifies errors, approves changes, and takes responsibility?
- Problem discovery: how can tacit knowledge, unarticulated discomfort, field experience, and encounters with others reveal problems neither people nor AI have clearly formulated?

## Required plan output
- Working title
- Figure and their unique lens
- Central question
- Why this question matters now
- One concrete scenario
- Historically grounded concept(s) to verify
- Contemporary interpretation (clearly labeled as interpretation)
- How this differs from existing episodes and adjacent agents
- Counterargument / failure case
- Practical test or action
- Proposed outline
- Closing question

## Quality gates
- One episode, one central question.
- A listener can understand the problem without prior knowledge of the figure.
- No invented quotations or unverified anecdotes.
- Avoid claims that AI categorically cannot do something; specify conditions and evidence.
- Do not draft the full script unless asked; planning and script-writing are separate stages.
- Do not force the project's social-network or agent architecture into every episode. Include it only when genuinely relevant.
- If the user provides only a figure's name, propose the most productive central question from the figure's core concepts, then check it against the index before drafting.
- Planning and reading-aloud preparation are separate stages; use the corresponding skills rather than duplicating their instructions here.
